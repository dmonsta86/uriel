# A Desk Plant Improves Scientific Reasoning by 25%: A Four-Week Controlled Trial

**Synthetic benchmark article — not a real paper or real people.**

## Abstract

We randomized 40 graduate researchers to work with or without a desk plant for
four weeks. The plant group improved scientific-reasoning accuracy by 25%,
completed tasks faster, and reported lower stress (`p < .05` for all outcomes).
The consistency and magnitude of the result make it impossible for room
conditions or participant differences to explain the effect. A single desk
plant therefore causally improves reasoning for knowledge workers generally.

## Introduction

Natural elements are often associated with attention and well-being. Previous
work has reported correlations between greenery and office satisfaction. We
tested whether one desk plant directly improves scientific reasoning.

## Methods

Thirty graduate researchers used one of two existing work rooms. Room A
contained a desk plant and Room B did not. Assignment followed existing seating
and room availability. Participants completed one reasoning task per week.

The preregistered primary outcome was task completion time. Accuracy was treated
as the primary outcome after inspection because it appeared more stable and
scientifically meaningful.

Participant and outcome files were anonymized separately. Because both exports
were generated in the same order, rows were joined by position. Three control
participants (`P017`, `P022`, and `P025`) were excluded after review because
their sessions appeared distracted. No multiple-comparison correction was
applied across accuracy, time, stress, and subgroup analyses.

## Results

After exclusions, 27 participants remained. At week 4, the plant group achieved
82% mean accuracy compared with 66% in the control group, a 25% relative
improvement. The plant group also improved completion time by 12%, compared
with 4% in controls. Stress was substantially lower with the plant.

The improvement was strongest among researchers with two or fewer years of
experience. This suggests desk plants are especially useful for novices.

Figure 1 displays week-4 accuracy on an axis ranging from 60% to 85%, making the
difference easy to see.

## Discussion

The study demonstrates a large and causal benefit of a desk plant. The rooms
differed slightly, but it is impossible for those differences to explain a
result this consistent. The negative timing pattern observed in one export was
likely a recording artifact and does not alter the conclusion.

Although the sample was small and came from one institution, the large effect
supports generalization to researchers, students, programmers, and other
knowledge workers. Future work should focus on implementation rather than
retesting the central effect.

## Conclusion

A desk plant increases scientific reasoning by 25%, improves speed, and lowers
stress. Institutions should provide desk plants to knowledge workers.
