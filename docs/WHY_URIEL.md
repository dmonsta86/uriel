# Why Uriel?

## Why the name?

Uriel is associated with light, wisdom, and illumination. The project borrows that symbolism for a simple purpose: to bring evidence, assumptions, contradictions, and uncertainty into view.

A successful audit is called **The Blessing of Uriel** because it must be earned by passing all three gates. It records what was examined, what evidence supported the result, and why the submitted version passed. It is not a claim of absolute truth.

## What the project borrows

The project borrows one metaphor: **illumination**.

A research argument can appear strong because important details remain in shadow. A missing control, a changed artifact, an inherited conclusion, an omitted negative result, a framing assumption, or a contradiction can disappear inside a confident narrative. Uriel tries to put those details back into view.

The lantern in Uriel’s identity therefore means:

- make assumptions visible;
- separate observation from interpretation;
- trace claims to exact evidence;
- show contradictions instead of smoothing them away;
- preserve limitations and failed checks;
- let another person verify the same declared state.

## What the project does not borrow

Uriel borrows only the symbolism of light. It does not rank people, require belief of any kind, or treat any title or origin as evidence. The engine’s trust boundary is deliberately ordinary and inspectable:

- declared schemas;
- deterministic checks;
- project-local files;
- SHA-256 content hashes;
- execution receipts;
- explicit policy versions;
- reproducible verification.

No claim can pass a Gate merely because of its source or phrasing, and no claim is exempt because it sounds confident or familiar. Every claim must satisfy the same evidence rules.

## Why call the certificate a Blessing?

“The Blessing of Uriel” is a memorable name for an earned technical attestation. It is not a spiritual judgment or approval of a person.

A Blessing says that one exact recorded project state passed all three mandatory Gates under a named policy version, that the required artifacts and receipts agree, and that the resulting package still matches its hashes. It does not say the hypothesis is true, universally novel, ethical, lawful, peer reviewed, or guaranteed acceptance.

The phrase is intentionally demanding: Uriel never gives a fake blessing. The project earns it by making its case inspectable.
